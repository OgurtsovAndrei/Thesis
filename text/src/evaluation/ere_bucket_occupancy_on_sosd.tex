\paragraph{Observations.}
\texttt{sosd\_fb} and \texttt{sosd\_osm} stay well below saturation at
every $\mathcal{L}$. \texttt{sosd\_books} runs at ${\sim}42\%$ across
$\mathcal{L}$. \texttt{sosd\_wiki} fully saturates at $\mathcal{L} = 16$
($99\%$).
In all cases except \texttt{sosd\_wiki} at $\mathcal{L}=256$, bucket footprints fit L1d on modern x86 servers, typically $32$--$48$~KB.
The saturated wiki bucket ($121$~KB) fits L2.

When fill approaches $100\%$, the SODA hash has run out of resolution:
every key in a dense input region collapses into a single ARE block, and
the bucket-search cost for those queries becomes $O(2^w)$ rather than
$O(1)$ on average. For \texttt{sosd\_wiki} this means binary search on a
fully packed bucket of up to ${\sim}6 \times 10^4$ keys ($121$~KB at
$\mathcal{L} = 256$) --- still bounded by $w$ iterations and cache-resident
in L2 (Section~\ref{subsec:block-sizes-and-lookup-strategy}),
but the buckets are no longer lightly loaded as the SODA construction assumes.
