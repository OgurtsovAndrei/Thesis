\section{Grafite: A Closer Look}
\label{sec:grafite-deep-dive}

Section~\ref{sec:existing-work} introduces the Grafite construction:
locality-preserving hash, Elias--Fano storage of hash codes, the
Theorem~3.4 space bound. We focus here on three angles relevant to
this thesis: graceful degradation on long queries, an implicit
universe definition that diverges from our Adaptive Filter
(Section~\ref{sec:adaptive}), and a fallback path our CGo wrapper
installs.

\subsection{Parameter Choices and Their Consequences}
\label{subsec:grafite-params}

Queries longer than the configured $\mathcal{L}$ degrade gracefully
along $\min\{1,\,\ell/2^{B-2}\}$; queries shorter get the design FPR
for free. On top of the budget identity, Theorem~3.4
of~\citep{grafite2024} carries the restriction
$\mathcal{L} \leq u\varepsilon/n$ --- equivalently
$r = n\mathcal{L}/\varepsilon \leq u$. Above this point the requested
hash range exceeds the universe, and the upstream constructor throws.
The authors use this throw deliberately, to direct the user to a
lossless encoding (Elias--Fano over the raw keys, or any other exact
representation): at this regime an approximate fingerprint costs more
bits than storing keys exactly, so building the hash is wasteful.

\paragraph{Universe normalization.}
Grafite measures the universe as $\max(S)$ rather than
$\max(S) - \min(S)$: keys are passed unshifted into both the hash and
the Elias--Fano builder, and the budget check fires on the absolute
maximum, not on the spread. Our Adaptive Filter
(Section~\ref{sec:adaptive}) subtracts $\min(S)$ first. When $\min(S)$
is far from zero, the normalized universe is strictly smaller --- the
Adaptive filter then enters exact mode on a wider parameter range,
and the resulting Elias--Fano encoding is smaller once both are in
exact mode.

\subsection{Lossless Elias--Fano Fallback}
\label{subsec:grafite-fallback}

We make the mode-selection automatic: our CGo wrapper catches the
throw and substitutes a direct \texttt{grafite::ef\_sux\_vector} over
the deduplicated input keys, giving deterministic $\mathrm{FPR} = 0$
at $n \cdot (2 + \log_2(\max(S)/n)) + \text{select-index overhead}$
bits. Grafite then remains comparable across the full budget axis
instead of failing on its tightest-$\varepsilon$ end. The next chapter
generalises this single-segment exact-mode trigger into a per-cluster
mechanism via segmentation.
